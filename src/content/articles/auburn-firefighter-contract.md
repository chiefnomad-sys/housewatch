A top-step firefighter in Auburn, N.Y., now makes $90,041 a year. That figure comes from the new contract between the city and Auburn Professional Firefighters Local 1446, the union for the city's career fire department. The city council approved it 3-2 on Thursday, Nov. 12, after rejecting the same agreement on Sept. 24.

The deal runs from July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2029, so the first raise is retroactive. The union had already ratified it. For crews elsewhere, the trade is the story: Auburn's firefighters gave up six firefighter positions, two seats per shift and a bigger share of the health premium for 3% raises, and kept their no-layoff clause. Other small career departments short on cash may see the same offer at the table.

## What crews get

**Raises.** 3% in each of the three years. The salary schedule runs eight steps, from $50,648 at the start to $90,041 at the top, The Citizen reported from the proposed terms. Under the old contract, the top step was $87,418. That works out to about $2,600 more a year at the top, or roughly $1.26 an hour on Auburn's rotation, which the old contract says averages about 40 hours a week. If the next two raises land on the same schedule, the top step reaches about $95,500 by July 2028.

**Job protection.** The contract keeps language saying positions "will not be left vacant, nor abolished due to economic or budgetary constraints." The city can still hold off on hiring until the next recruit class at the New York State Fire Academy.

That clause has been a fight all year. In the spring, while the city weighed an 8% cut across departments, Fire Chief Mark Fritz told the council a cut that size would eliminate nine firefighter positions, according to The Citizen. Councilor Craig Diego called the existing agreements "iron-clad," the paper reported. Local 1446 said in a Facebook post that its contract had been negotiated and agreed to by the city.

## What crews give up

**Seats on the rig.** Minimum staffing per shift drops from 14 to 12. Below 12, the city has to call people in on overtime. Total contractual staffing drops from 71 to 65: 46 firefighters, 12 lieutenants and four captains on the line, plus three day staff. The old contract had 52 firefighters on that line, so all six positions come out of the firefighter rank.

**Health insurance.** The employee share of premiums goes up 1.25 points a year: 16.25% this past July, 17.5% in July 2027 and 18.75% in July 2028. Under the old contract most members paid 15%. The new deal also drops an incentive tied to the preferred provider organization (PPO) plan. Premium dollar amounts haven't been reported, so the per-paycheck cost isn't public yet.

## Why the vote flipped

Mayor James Giannettino Jr. voted no in September and yes on Nov. 12. He said keeping the 14-person minimum while the contract went to arbitration would mean more overtime, with estimates running from hundreds of thousands of dollars to as much as $2 million depending on how long arbitration lasted, FingerLakes1 reported. He also said the deal falls short of the 25% premium share he wanted. "I want to emphasize that this is an opportunity, not a guarantee," he said, according to FingerLakes1.

Councilors Terry Cuddy and Christina Calarco voted yes both times. Diego and Rhoda Overstreet-Wilson voted no both times. Diego pointed to a 22.8% jump in health insurance costs and a preliminary estimate of about $710,000 over budget, and said nothing had been budgeted for raises or back pay. City Manager Jennifer Haines said the insurance cost is spread across several city funds and isn't a calculated general fund deficit. In an earlier memo, she said the city's savings come from the lower staffing, the higher employee premium shares and dropping the PPO incentive.

Local 1446 President Ryan Guerrette told the council that eight fire positions had already been eliminated or left unfilled in earlier budget cuts. Fritz put the department's overtime down to vacancies, injuries and recruits still in the academy.

## What happens next

The mayor said three firefighters are expected to finish academy training by the end of November. The council already moved $216,799 from reserves in October to reach and keep 65 firefighters, covering two new hires and one who replaces a retiring firefighter.

Comptroller Mary Beth Leeson said the city's health insurance estimate will be clearer after open enrollment ends in December.
