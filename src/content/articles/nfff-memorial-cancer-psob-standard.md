A firefighter who dies of one of the covered cancers, in any state, career or volunteer, can now be considered for the memorial on the same terms. Until now, where they served mattered. Ten states account for 71% of the nearly 400 firefighters who died of cancer and have been honored or approved by the foundation since 2019, and 13 states have yet to have a single firefighter added for a cancer line-of-duty death.

On Nov. 23, the foundation, whose memorial stands at the National Fire Academy in Emmitsburg, Md., announced that its board had changed the rule behind those numbers. For cancer deaths on or after Jan. 1, 2020, the test is now an approved claim under the Justice Department's Public Safety Officers' Benefits program, known as PSOB. A firefighter's state no longer decides it.

Before the change, the foundation needed documentation that a cancer death met the presumption law of the state where the firefighter served. It started considering cancer deaths in 2018, and with no federal standard for firefighter cancer outside 9/11-related illness, state law was the only yardstick it had. A firefighter whose state covered the cancer could make the memorial. One whose state didn't, or whose state law left out volunteers, couldn't.

The federal yardstick came from the Honoring Our Fallen Heroes Act, which became law in December 2025 as part of the annual defense bill. It added 20 cancers to PSOB and treats them as job-related for firefighters, so survivors no longer have to tie the cancer to one specific exposure. It covers career and volunteer firefighters and reaches back to deaths on or after Jan. 1, 2020. The International Association of Fire Fighters (IAFF) puts the federal death benefit at more than $461,000.

The change also reaches volunteers some state laws left out. "Many state presumption laws limit or exclude volunteers. PSOB does not," Chief Kevin D. Quinn, a foundation board member and immediate past chairman of the National Volunteer Fire Council, said in the release.

## What a family or department has to do

The memorial now hangs on the PSOB claim, so the claim comes first. Under the foundation's updated criteria:

- The foundation can hold a cancer case open while the Justice Department decides. If PSOB finds a line-of-duty death, the firefighter is eligible. If PSOB says no, the firefighter is not.
- If no PSOB claim is filed within one year of the death, the foundation closes the file as "Not Eligible."
- Paperwork for the 2028 Roll of Honor opens Jan. 1, 2027.

On the PSOB side, the IAFF's guide to the expansion says the cancer has to be diagnosed after at least five years in a covered fire or EMS role, and for retirees no more than 15 years after retirement. For deaths from Jan. 1, 2020, through Dec. 18, 2025, the IAFF says claims must be filed by Dec. 18, 2028. Later deaths fall under PSOB's usual three-year window.

The foundation helps families file PSOB claims for cancer deaths through its Local Assistance State Team program, which keeps an online directory on firehero.org.

## What doesn't change

Firefighters already on the memorial stay, and everyone approved for the 2027 Roll of Honor will be added in May 2027 as planned. Cancer deaths from 2018 through 2026 that were already reported to the foundation will still be judged under state presumption rules.

The federal route has limits. Some state laws cover cancers missing from the federal list, and PSOB decisions can be slow, Firehouse reported. The law requires the Justice Department to review the cancer list at least once every three years, according to the National Volunteer Fire Council.

The foundation already uses PSOB findings for most other presumptive causes, including COVID-19 and 9/11-related illness, Firehouse reported. Cancer was the holdout because there was no federal standard to use until last December.

For a department with a cancer death since 2020 that it has not yet reported to the foundation, the order is now set: file the PSOB claim, then send the approval to the foundation. The first firefighters added that way go on the memorial in May 2028.
