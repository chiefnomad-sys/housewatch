Around 6 a.m. on Wednesday, Nov. 18, many people in Glace Bay heard it. One nearby resident told CTV that her clock read 5:57 when "my bed shook."

The blast came from Pizza 67, at 130 Commercial Street near Main Street, in the Cape Breton Regional Municipality. "There was an explosion in downtown Glace Bay at Pizza 67," Cst. Gary Fraser of the Cape Breton Regional Police told CTV. The pizza shop was on the ground floor. The upper floors were apartments, about 20 of them according to CBC.

CBRM spokesperson Christina Lamey laid out the order of events for Global News: "An explosion was reported by many witnesses to the scene and followed by a fire that swept through the building." Police said the front of the building collapsed.

## In the back, then out

The front was gone as a way in. "We could not make entry on the front," Glace Bay Volunteer Fire Department Chief John Chant told CTV. "The building was collapsing around us."

So crews went to the back. "We started an entry on the back as we were told there was people inside," Chant told CBC. When the wall began to collapse, he pulled them out.

That's the call every officer hopes never to make and has to be ready to make anyway: a report of people inside, a building that has already taken an explosion, and a structure that's telling you it's done. Chant summed up the call for CTV: "It's one of those calls that you know could be a mass casualty event."

He's been at this a long time. "I've never seen this in my 30 years in the fire service," Chant told CBC.

## Sixteen departments and a theatre next door

According to CBC, crews from 16 fire departments across Cape Breton responded, along with police and paramedics. Other reports put it at "more than a dozen" or "several," so the exact count may move.

Next door, at 116 Commercial Street, is the Savoy Theatre, a 1927 building and the largest soft-seat theatre on Cape Breton Island. Crews put water on it to protect it, CBC reported. It came through with smoke and water damage, according to its executive director, Pam Leader. The theatre posted: "The Savoy is okay." The blast also broke windows in nearby buildings, including across the street.

CBRM Mayor Cecil Clarke credited the firefighters, "whose extraordinary efforts contained the blaze to a single structure," in a statement quoted by CBC.

An excavator came in and took down what was left of the building around 10:30 a.m., CTV reported. Crews were still putting out fire at 11 a.m., per Global News, and spent the afternoon working through debris to make sure it was out.

## The count, and the cleanup

Lamey said about 17 people were injured, 14 of them taken to Glace Bay Hospital, and that the injuries were minor. "We at this time do not have a report of any fatalities," she told Global News. No firefighter injuries were reported. MLA John White told CBC all 24 residents had been accounted for.

More than 1,600 Nova Scotia Power customers lost power around 6:30 a.m., with power back a few hours later, according to CTV. The area around Main, Commercial and Union streets was expected to stay closed for 72 hours because of debris. A comfort centre opened at the Glace Bay Miners' Forum, and the Canadian Red Cross and the province were helping displaced residents find housing.

As of Wednesday, officials had not said what caused the explosion. Lamey told CBC it was "too early to know what caused the explosion, collapse or fire."

## The part crews will recognize

Nobody wants to be the officer who backs a crew out of a building with people reported inside. Glace Bay's crews went in from the side they could, got out when the wall started to go, and then did the rest of the job from outside: protect the 1927 theatre next door, keep it to one building, and let the excavator finish what the explosion started. Seventeen people hurt and no deaths reported, from a blast in an occupied apartment building at 6 in the morning, is about as good as that call gets.
