If you work for the Kenner Fire Department and you are in your first three years, or past your 23rd, the city's 2023 pay plan cut you out of the yearly 2% raise.

The plan limited the department's annual 2% raise to firefighters with three to 23 years on the job, according to WDSU and Fox 8. Before that, the raise went to everyone. On Nov. 20 the City Council voted 5-1, with one abstention, to put it back for all full-time firefighters, starting with their first full year and running the length of a career. The raises would take effect in early 2027.

For a firefighter, the question is what happens when voters pass a tax for fire pay and the people at both ends of the seniority list still don't see the raise.

## The money voters already approved

In November 2023, Kenner voters approved a larger dedicated fire millage, 18.2 mills replacing a 7.4-mill tax that dated to 1983. Its stated uses started with firefighter wages to improve retention, followed by station work, PPE, equipment and training, Fire Apparatus Magazine reported before the vote.

Councilman Joey LaHatte, who introduced the ordinance, pointed to the millage. "The public passed the millage. They're paying the money," he said, as reported by WGNO.

Over the summer, LaHatte pushed measures to move $250,000 from the police budget to the fire department to pay for the restored raises, Fox 8 reported. The administration has not paid that money out, WWL reported. WGNO reported that about 25 firefighters would be affected by the change.

At the meeting, one resident asked about a raise of "27 cents an hour" for beginning firefighters, WWL reported. That is small in year one. It compounds every year for a whole career, and under the 2023 plan it doesn't start until year three.

## Who left

The Kenner Firefighter Association says 27 firefighters have resigned since the millage passed, not counting retirements. Association President Nicholas Zalva said, "Every departure takes experience and potential future leadership with it," according to WWL.

Capt. Thomas Caragliano backed the ordinance. "We're not asking for special treatment. We're asking for fair recognition of years of service," Caragliano said, as reported by WDSU. Caragliano also said many of those who left went for better pay, WGNO reported.

Councilman George Branigan voted no and Councilwoman Dee Dunn abstained, Fox 8 reported. WDSU said both cited the legal concerns raised by the city attorney.

## The mayor's side

Glaser points to what the millage has already bought. He said starting pay for Kenner firefighters is up 46% since the tax passed, which he called a $7,000 raise, and that one new station is open and another is planned, according to WWL and WGNO. WWL reported he also cited new trucks and equipment, and said Kenner pays state supplemental pay from a firefighter's first day rather than after the first year.

His objection is timing. The association sued the city in 2023 over wages and pension benefits, and mediation in that case is set for Jan. 12. "This is not the appropriate time to do it," Glaser said of the ordinance, as reported by Fox 8.

City Attorney Michelle Dufrene said the power to set these salaries rests with the mayor, and warned the vote could bring more litigation, WGNO reported. The city attorney also said the raises could complicate the pending lawsuit. LaHatte, an attorney himself, argues that Section 3.09 of the charter lets the council carry out the pay plan, though not set pay for individual employees.

## What happens next

The ordinance now goes to Glaser, who has a seven-day review period, according to WDSU, and who hinted to Fox 8 that he would veto it. If he does, it returns to the council, which can try to override. LaHatte has said he is open to an override or to going to court.

Then comes Jan. 12, when the city and the association sit down in mediation on the lawsuit. Glaser has said he is waiting on that mediation before acting on firefighter pay.
