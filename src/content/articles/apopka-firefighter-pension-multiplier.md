A firefighter who puts in 20 years with the Apopka Fire Department used to retire on 65% of final average pay. As of this week, the same 20 years is worth 80%.

That's the math behind Ordinance 3182, which the Apopka City Commission adopted unanimously on Wednesday, Nov. 18, The Apopka Chief reported. The ordinance rewrites the firefighters' retirement plan to match the three-year contract the city ratified in September with Apopka Professional Firefighters Association Local 5293. Apopka is a city of about 66,000 in Orange County, and both sides have said in public that this deal came together without a fight. For any local heading into bargaining, that makes it a concrete benchmark: a flat 4% and 20-and-out, written into city code.

## What changed

The old formula paid 3% of average final compensation for each of the first 10 years, 3.5% for years 11 through 20, and 4% for each year after that, according to The Apopka Chief's Nov. 13 report on the first reading. The new one pays a flat 4% for every year of credited service.

On a final average of $90,000, that's the difference between $58,500 a year and $72,000 a year at the 20-year mark.

Normal retirement is now 20 years of service at any age, or age 45 with at least 10 years. The changes cover firefighters who retire or enter the Deferred Retirement Option Plan on or after Oct. 1, 2026. The 4% multiplier is what the union asked for when talks opened in June, along with a pension in line with the city's police plan, Spectrum News reported.

## The rest of the contract

The contract runs from Oct. 1, 2026, through Sept. 30, 2029. In year one, every employee got a $12,000 base pay increase, up to the top of their range, The Apopka Chief reported in September. That's $1,000 a month before taxes. The firefighter/EMT range is now $66,063 to $102,818, and engineers run $74,984 to $111,433. The second and third years carry 3% across-the-board raises plus merit increases of up to 3%.

Paramedic certification pay goes from $4 to $5 an hour, which the paper said reaches about 100 firefighters. Field training officers, inspectors, instructors and Special Hazards Operations Team members get added pay, and rank-to-rank promotions come with a 10% bump.

For scale, Orange County approved a contract with the Orange County Fire Fighters Association, Local 2057, in May that set starting pay for a county firefighter at $59,072, or $62,025 after orientation, News 6 reported. Apopka's firefighter/EMT range now starts at $66,063. Before the raise, Fire Chief Wil Sanchez told Spectrum News that Apopka's wages sat in the lower-middle range of the surrounding departments.

## How it got done

Local 5293 members voted on the contract Sept. 17 to 19, and the commission ratified it unanimously Sept. 24. The talk at that meeting was about how little friction there was. "It's night and day from where we were last time in the process," Local 5293 President Kyle Lampp said, according to The Apopka Chief. Mayor Nick Nesta told Sanchez the process had shown bargaining "doesn't need to be adversarial."

At the Nov. 18 meeting, Mike Duran spoke in favor of the pension ordinance. His son, Apopka firefighter Austin Duran, died in 2022 after an on-duty injury, and Duran has since pushed for firefighter safety and staffing, including through the 2650 Foundation he helped start in his son's honor. "It is extremely important that we do this because we need to retain these firefighters," he told the commission.

## What it costs, and what's still open

Sanchez told the commission the pension change costs up to about $1.2 million this fiscal year, based on the actuarial study done during bargaining. Nesta said the contract was fully funded in the budget the city had already adopted.

The cost after this year hasn't been set. Commissioner Sam Ruth, who seconded the motion, asked what the next two years would look like. "I'm just trying to get some real numbers," he said. Pension board chairman Mark Fry explained that the plan gets an actuarial review every year and that changes in the city's contribution are smoothed over several years.

For Apopka firefighters, the next number to watch is the first annual actuarial review under the new formula. That review is where the commission gets the real numbers Ruth asked for, and where the city's contribution for the years after this one starts to take shape.
