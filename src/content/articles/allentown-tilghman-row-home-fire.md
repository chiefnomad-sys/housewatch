The roofs at 724 and 726 Tilghman Street came down while Allentown firefighters were still working the block. "We've had multiple roof collapses in two of the involved homes," Capt. Jonathan Hammel, the department's captain of public affairs, said at the scene, The Morning Call reported.

The call came in about 12:55 p.m. on Sunday, Nov. 22, as a house fire in the 700 block of Tilghman Street. The first crews found several attached homes already burning, with the fire centered at the rear of the buildings. The response went to five alarms.

Every older city has blocks like this one, and every crew that works them knows the problem: once fire gets into the hidden spaces of attached homes, it can run down the row while companies are still fighting it at the first address. That is what happened on Tilghman Street. Officials said the older construction left hidden voids that complicated the fight and led to the extensive damage, Fox 29 reported.

## Inside and on the roofs

Companies attacked from inside the homes and from the rooftops in heavy smoke, and NBC10 video showed firefighters working up on the roofs. The south side of the block was evacuated as a precaution.

Then two of the buildings became too unstable to enter, and crews had to fight those from the outside, according to The Morning Call. With a roof gone, the heavy fire and thick black smoke got harder to contain, 6abc reported, and the worst of the damage ran along the backs of the homes and the rooflines.

It took about three hours of work before crews made real headway, Fox 29 reported, and the fire was mostly under control by Sunday afternoon. Crews were still on scene at 6 p.m. searching for hot spots and watching the damaged homes, according to 6abc.

## Who came

Allentown sent most of its apparatus and recalled off-duty firefighters from home, Fox 29 reported. Mutual aid companies came from Bethlehem, Bethlehem Township, South Whitehall Township, Lower Macungie Township, Emmaus, Whitehall, Catasauqua and Northampton, according to Fox 29 and The Morning Call, along with an emergency management drone team.

Three firefighters had minor injuries and went to the hospital, and all have been released, NBC10 and Fox 29 reported. Allentown paramedics treated several residents at the scene, The Morning Call reported, and NBC10 reported that no residents were injured.

## The block

Hammel told WFMZ that seven homes will need restoration before the people who live there can move back in.

The Morning Call reported that 42 people were forced out at first and 32 would be out longer. NBC10 and WFMZ put the number still displaced at 26. The American Red Cross is helping families. WFMZ reported that local groups, including Community Action Lehigh Valley and the United Way, are coordinating help, and Second Harvest Food Bank is handing out boxes of food meant to last about three days.

## Cause and what comes next

The cause is still under investigation. Hammel said the fire may have started outside, at the rear of the homes near 722 Tilghman Street, Fox 29 reported, but told NBC10 that investigators have not yet pinned down exactly what caused it or where it started. Hammel told 6abc that so far the department has no reason to believe the fire was suspicious.

The Allentown fire marshal is working the investigation with the Reading Fire Department and the federal Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, The Morning Call reported. Plans for the worst-hit homes are still being worked out, Fox 29 reported, and those plans will shape when the families of the 700 block get to go home.
